I am Hande Sodacı, a behavioral researcher with a PhD in Cognitive Psychology from Koç University (2020–2026) and over ten years of experience designing controlled experiments and making sense of real behavioral data. My work looks at how people change the way they communicate — what they say, when, and how much detail they give — when talking to a robot or another conversational agent instead of another person.

**Background.** I did my BA in Psychology, with a certificate in Linguistics, at Boğaziçi University, then a research master's in Language and Communication at Radboud University in the Netherlands. During that time I worked as a research assistant at Tilburg University on the EU Horizon 2020 [L2TOR](/academia/) project, designing a personalized language-learning study for bilingual children using a social robot, which is where my interest in human–robot interaction started. I later coordinated a TÜBİTAK-funded longitudinal study at MEF University before starting my PhD at Koç University.

**What I actually do.** I design and run controlled experiments (my dissertation work covers 200+ participants and thousands of individual responses), and I use statistical modeling in R to find real, sometimes subtle, patterns in how people behave, including when a result does not turn out the way I expected. I also build the technical side of these studies, including programming robot interactions, and write up findings for both academic and general audiences.

**Where I am headed.** I am moving that experimental mindset into product and research roles — work that tests how people actually behave and turns that into something useful, at a faster pace than academic research allows.

You can see my research and past projects under [projects](/projects/talking-to-robots/) in the nav above, and my full background on my [resume](/assets/HS_resume_202609.pdf). Feel free to [get in touch](/contact/).
